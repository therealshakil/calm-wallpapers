# Curation — how a wallpaper earns its place

The set stays small. Subtraction is the default (Article VIII): a picture is removed as readily as
added, and "no" is the resting answer to "should this one go in?". This is the short bar a wallpaper
clears before it joins `wallpapers/`.

## The bar

A wallpaper earns its place when all of these are true:

- **It recedes.** You can work over it without noticing it. If it pulls the eye back, it's a print, not
  a wallpaper — it doesn't go in.
- **Monochrome, warm.** Ink on warm paper or bone-white on soft off-black. No colour, no gradient, no
  pure black or white.
- **Mostly empty.** The subject is small and low; most of the frame is quiet space, with room for
  desktop icons.
- **Quiet, not clever.** Warmth over wit. No text, no logo, no joke that wears thin by the third day.
- **Part of the family.** It sits beside the others as one hand's work (the `--sref` discipline in
  [`recipe.md`](./recipe.md) does most of this).

If you're unsure, it's a no. The set is better small.

## How it's stored

- `wallpapers/light/` — ink on warm paper.
- `wallpapers/dark/` — bone-white on soft off-black.
- Name plainly by subject and ratio: `sleeping-cat-16x10.png`. No dates, no version numbers, no `v2` —
  if a piece is replaced, replace the file.

## Removing one

Removing is not a loss; it's the set staying calm. If a wallpaper stops feeling quiet, delete it. No
archive, no "deprecated" folder — that would just be a feed by another name (Article I).

---

## Constitution Check

1. **Serves:** VIII (subtract by default; a small set, easily pruned), I (no archive, no growing feed),
   VI (the bar is "it recedes").
2. **Violates:** none — the bar enforces monochrome, empty, and quiet; no surface here invites
   browsing.
3. **Subtracts:** the whole doc is a guard against accumulation — removing is framed as the calm act,
   not the loss.
4. **Calm:** "if you're unsure, it's a no" removes the pressure to keep everything; a small set is
   stated as the better outcome, not a shortfall.
